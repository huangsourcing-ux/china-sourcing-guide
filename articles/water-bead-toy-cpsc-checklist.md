# Water Bead Toy Imports from China: 2026 CPSC Checklist

> Published: July 18, 2026
> Updated: September 5, 2026
> Original: https://www.huangsourcing.com/water-bead-toy-cpsc-checklist

Check water bead toy size, acrylamide, warnings, CPC data, test identity, packaging, and cartons before paying or shipping China-sourced orders to the U.S.

For U.S.-bound water bead toys manufactured after March 12, 2026, final payment and pickup should wait until the importer-approved test plan, size-limit and acrylamide evidence, required warnings, Children’s Product Certificate data, and physical shipment all identify the same product and production version.

## Quick Answer

Use a two-part release gate. First, a CPSC-accepted laboratory and qualified compliance adviser should decide scope, sampling, testing, warnings, and certification. Then a China-side check can compare those approved references with the actual dry beads, contained-bead toys, retail packs, labels, cartons, lot records, and corrections. Hold release when the specialist decision or shipment match is unclear.

## September 2026 Public Case Update

On September 3, 2026, CPSC announced the recall of about 8,000 China-made GIHNJSI Squeezy Dumplings sold on Amazon. The notice says the contained water beads could create ingestion, choking, and intestinal-obstruction hazards, reports no incidents or injuries, and notes that the product had no markings while retail wording appeared on a removable label.

A separate CPSC warning that day expressly described another China-sold squishy bun toy as violating the mandatory standard for toys containing water beads. On September 4, UK OPSS published an independent report for a China-origin Squishy Dumpling D-9373 that could break apart and release water beads; that import was rejected at the border under UK requirements.

The buyer-side release lesson is to connect the qualified scope and test plan, contained beads, shell, seams, closures, product and pack warnings, SKU, lot, report, CPC data, and physical cartons. The cited records do not prove another product, supplier, factory, formulation, or lot has the same issue, and UK law does not determine U.S. compliance.
